\title{One Operator Is Not Enough, Yet: Cost, Learnability, and Guidance
Collapse in a Single-Operator Representation of Mathematics at Fixed Scale}
\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
One binary operator can, in principle, represent all of elementary mathematics:
Odrzywo{\l}ek~\cite{odrzywolek2026} shows every elementary function is
constructible from the constant~$1$ and $\eml(x,y)=\exp(x)-\ln(y)$, turning any
expression into a strict binary tree whose internal nodes all carry one
operation. Such a single-operator representation is maximally regular---a reason
to consider it for graph learning---but its evident cost is size. We measure that
cost exactly before asking whether the regularity helps a model, and we do so at
one fixed scale: a
deterministic, quality-audited corpus of $250{,}000$ expressions with frozen
splits. Compiling the corpus with the pinned official compiler yields a median
tree expansion factor $\alpha=40.66$ over the source AST (mean $952.1$, p99
$10{,}448.6$), and $0/250{,}000$ expressions pass the preregistered per-family
thresholds ($1.29$--$1.50$, against a whole-grammar counting break-even of
$\approx\!1.56$). Lossless DAG sharing compresses pure-EML trees $39.4\times$ on
average, yet the shared EML DAG remains larger than the \emph{uncompressed} AST
tree for every one of the $250{,}000$ expressions, the best remaining ratio being
exactly $8/7$. Bounded, verified equality saturation improves $23.9$--$27.6\%$ of
costed expressions at $2.4$--$2.8\%$ mean relative savings, conditional on
$60.7\%$ vocabulary coverage. We publish two preregistered nulls (a learned motif
vocabulary loses to an equal-budget frequent baseline; a neural cost ranker loses
to a structural heuristic) and one preregistered gate verdict, a \emph{fail},
reported with content hashes and not softened. We then release a frozen,
leakage-controlled learning apparatus and report its first authenticated verdicts.
The single-operator EML-DAG representation is \emph{trainable}: on binary
equivalence, two of three seeds reach a validation loss of ${\approx}0.53$ BCE,
below the $\ln 2\approx0.693$ chance floor, despite the $40\times$ expansion (the
third seed diverges, at $2.39$). Yet a goal-conditioned value head
\emph{collapses}: it reaches a low validation MAE (${\approx}0.83$) while ranking
at chance (test-IID Spearman ${\approx}0$) and emitting a \emph{constant}
out-of-distribution predictor, a guidance-signal failure that the rank and OOD
diagnostics expose but MAE alone would hide. The study estimates no scaling laws,
and frontier LLMs appear only as external context.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Is one operator enough to represent mathematics for a learning model? A
single-operator representation is maximally regular: a graph network reading a
pure-EML term never has to learn \emph{what} an operator is, only \emph{where}
things connect. The objection is size. Lowering even $\sin(x)$ to pure EML
produces hundreds of nodes, and the standard reply is that sharing (DAGs),
macros, and semantic rewriting could close the gap. Whether they do is an
empirical question that, to our knowledge, had not been measured exactly at a
nontrivial scale---and whether whatever gap remains buys anything as inductive
bias is a second question the measurement alone cannot settle. We answer the
first exactly and take the first authenticated steps on the second: the cost is
disqualifying and unrecovered, the representation is nonetheless trainable, and
the guidance signal we train on it collapses.

This paper is deliberately narrow: a \emph{structural feasibility study} under
preregistered thresholds and complete denominators that publishes null and failed
outcomes alongside positives, separating what the representation \emph{costs}
structurally (measured in full at production scale) from whether that cost
\emph{buys} anything for learning (which we do not fully answer). We fit no scaling
law, run no leaderboard, and use no frontier LLM as a controlled baseline; every
number below matches a committed per-goal summary exactly.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}
\item An exact measurement of the single-operator representation's structural
cost at production ($250{,}000$-expression) scale: median expansion
$\alpha=40.66$, with $0/250{,}000$ expressions below the preregistered per-family
thresholds (C1--C3, \S\ref{sec:results}).
\item Two preregistered nulls and one published gate verdict, a \textbf{fail}
(Gate~G10), reported with content hashes and not softened (C4/C9,
\S\ref{sec:gate} and~\S\ref{sec:results}).
\item The first authenticated learning verdicts for the representation: a
\emph{trainable} single-operator encoder (equivalence validation loss
${\approx}0.53$ BCE, below the $\ln 2$ chance floor) alongside a \emph{diagnosed}
value-head collapse (low MAE, chance-level ranking) (C5/C7, \S\ref{sec:pending}).
\item A transferable methodological lesson---regression error is not ranking
utility for a learned value function---and a frozen, verifier-gated,
leakage-controlled apparatus released for reuse (\S\S\ref{sec:models}--\ref{sec:tasks}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Representations, immutable data, and hypotheses}
\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Representations}
Every representation we compare derives from the same source expression, so
differences between them are differences of encoding rather than of content. The
structural study (\S\ref{sec:results}) uses four: the ordered source AST as a
tree and as a hash-consed DAG~\cite{ershov1958,filliatre2006}, and the
official-v4 pure-EML term as a tree and as a hash-consed DAG. Writing $\alpha$
for the ratio of exact pure-EML tree nodes to source AST nodes, the tree pair
fixes $\alpha$ and the DAG pair fixes what maximal sharing recovers from it.

The learning grid consumes a different and smaller set, because two of the
structural objects are too large to encode at production scale. Four graph
channels enter the six-arm equivalence grid (\S\ref{sec:tasks}): the AST DAG,
the pure-EML DAG, a macro-derived frequent-motif DAG, and a train-only motif-AST
fair control. Two non-graph controls complete the grid: a prefix token sequence
over the same expressions, and a floor of $19$ predeclared surface counts behind
a single linear layer. The floor exists to measure how much of the task surface
counting already solves, and its feature list is frozen in code and asserted by
tests, so no reader can mistake it for a weak learned model.

Macro and motif graphs are lossless DAGs over approved source constructions, not
pure-EML graphs: each macro node carries \texttt{is\_pure\_eml = false}, its
pure-EML expansion cost is separate metadata never reported as macro size or
$\alpha$, and the runner flags whether any structural metric may be compared
across channels. All graphs follow the standard term-graph
model~\cite{barendregt1987}, with retained repeated references (so $f(x,x)$
carries two child references to one interned node) and a canonical node signature
over roots, edge directions and roles, values, and family; structural identity is
hash equality on that signature. Semantic equivalence is a separate relation,
established only by the verifier, and we mark each use.

\subsection{Fixed data foundation (C1)}
\label{sec:corpus}
The corpus contains $250{,}000$ expressions, unique under the canonical
\texttt{(domain\_mode, sympy\_srepr)} identity rather than up to semantic
equivalence, with frozen splits of
$175{,}000\,/\,25{,}000\,/\,25{,}000\,/\,25{,}000$
(train\,/\,validation\,/\,test\_iid\,/\,test\_ood) and six source families under
exact quotas: algebraic core ($70{,}000$), exp/log ($40{,}000$),
powers/division/rationals ($40{,}000$), trig/hyperbolic ($40{,}000$), mixed
elementary ($35{,}000$), and an OOD-stress family
($25{,}000$).\anchor{docs/goals/goal1/CORPUS\_QA.md;
docs/goals/goal2/GOAL2\_SUMMARY.md, ``Corpus and denominators'';
FINAL\_REPORT.md \#c1-goals-1-5-corpus (pre-authentication digest index).}
Generation is deterministic and QA-gated; manifests carry per-shard checksums,
the config hash, the seed, and a clean commit.

The held-out split and the OOD family coincide by construction: all $25{,}000$
OOD-stress rows are assigned to \texttt{test\_ood}. Their profile is a combined
stress over size, depth, variable count, and composition---not a depth-controlled
holdout, and we do not report it as one. This study is fixed to the immutable v1
foundation; regenerating the Goals~1--5 inputs would invalidate the comparison it
exists to support.

\subsection{Predeclared outcome families and hypotheses}
Outcome families were declared before the production runs and reported
separately---\emph{structural} (tree/DAG size and depth, pure-EML $\alpha$, macro
size, dictionary-inclusive motif description length~\cite{rissanen1978}),
\emph{predictive} (the six-arm equivalence grid), \emph{rewrite/proof}, \emph{SR},
and \emph{grammar-v2 conformance}. No pooled score exists across families or
across channels within the structural family: the runner names every structural
metric per channel and the plotting code refuses a shared $\alpha$ column.

Three preregistered hypotheses motivate the structural program. \textbf{H1}: raw
pure-EML expansion stays below the per-family counting thresholds
$\alpha_{\text{tree}} < 1 + \ln K / \ln 4L$ (bounded vocabulary $K$, literal bound
$L$; range $1.291$--$1.500$). \textbf{H2}: exact structural sharing closes
whatever gap H1 leaves, so a shared pure-EML DAG reaches parity with the
uncompressed AST tree. \textbf{H3}: verified semantic rewriting and learned motif
vocabularies close the residual. Structural statistics use all $250{,}000$ rows;
semantic auditing draws on a separate bounded sample and we never extrapolate it
(\S\ref{sec:limits}).


\section{Compact models and fair compute}
\label{sec:models}

This section and \S\ref{sec:tasks} describe committed, CI-tested apparatus; no
number in them is a result (the learning verdicts are in \S\ref{sec:results}). We
describe it because its frozen design, not its output, is a contribution.

\subsection{A compact GINE-style graph encoder}
The encoder is a compact variant of GINE~\cite{xu2019powerful,hu2020strategies}:
three layers, hidden width $64$, and about $0.2$M parameters. It uses residual
connections, GraphNorm or LayerNorm, dropout, a two-layer FFN, and sum pooling.
For a message edge $u\rightarrow v$ the edge feature and update are
\begin{equation}
a_{uv}=E_{\mathrm{dir}}(d_{uv})+E_{\mathrm{slot}}(s_{uv})+E_{\mathrm{role}}(r_{uv}),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widetilde h_v^{(\ell+1)}
&=\operatorname{MLP}_{\ell}\big((1+\epsilon_\ell)\,h_v^{(\ell)}+m_v^{(\ell)}\big),\\
m_v^{(\ell)}&=\sum_{u\in\mathcal N(v)}
\operatorname{ReLU}\big(h_u^{(\ell)}+W_\ell\,a_{uv}\big),
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
with the ordering fixed by the frozen implementation. The four arms differ only
in their input (AST-DAG, pure-EML-DAG, frequent-motif DAG, motif-AST). Every
compressed graph is forced to reconstruct.

\subsection{Task composition and controls}
For symmetric equivalence classification, the shared endpoint encoders produce
graph embeddings $g_a,g_b$, which can be combined as
\begin{equation}
p(a,b)=\big[\,g_a+g_b,\ \lvert g_a-g_b\rvert,\ g_a\odot g_b\,\big],
\label{eq:pair}
\end{equation}
symmetric in $a,b$ by construction. The transparent floor uses $19$ predeclared
surface counts that every learned arm must beat: operator, primitive, variable,
size, and depth counts. Vocabulary embeddings count toward the matched parameter
budget, since pure-EML label vocabularies differ per channel and shift the
totals.

\subsection{Compute matching and training}
\label{sec:compute}
Because pure-EML graphs are much larger than AST or macro graphs for the same
expression, equal parameters are \emph{not} equal compute. We therefore report,
jointly with every quality number, the full compute envelope: parameters, measured
and estimated FLOPs, wall time, peak host and device memory, GPU-hours,
graph/token size, effective examples and nodes per update, batch and accumulation
budgets, optimizer and step budget, early-stopping state, precision, and
deterministic settings. Hidden width and the virtual-node flag are frozen only by
a measured parameter/FLOP preflight on production vocabularies, which do not exist
yet, so the grid config records \texttt{pending\_preflight\_freeze} rather than a
guess (Table~\ref{tab:params}). We use three seeds and publish every seed row; the
mean never replaces the raw rows. The default profile is $2\times$H100 $80$\,GB.

\section{Bounded tasks and verification}
\label{sec:tasks}

We evaluate representation quality through bounded, verifier-gated tasks rather
than a single aggregate score. Every task has frozen identifiers, declared
budgets, retained failure states, and explicit denominators; unavailable cells are
reported as missing, not encoded as zero.

\subsection{Equivalence}

The production equivalence specification fixes
$50{,}000/5{,}000/5{,}000/5{,}000$ accepted pair records (train/val/IID/OOD). Each
accepted pair is a concrete, replayable rewrite trajectory---rule identifier,
direction, occurrence path, bindings, assumptions, source and successor
signatures, and separate verifier evidence---and source-, e-class-, and
trace-relative grouping prevents leakage across partitions, with rejected,
invalid, unsupported, and timeout candidates kept in typed ledgers. The build
aligns every endpoint across four graph channels (AST-DAG, strict pure-EML-DAG, a
macro-derived frequent-motif DAG, and a train-only AST motif control matched on
dictionary-entry count and MDL bits) plus sequence and feature-count controls;
IID, OOD-stress, depth-OOD, and family-OOD views never alter the base split
totals.

The pipeline has run end-to-end on a byte-identical $10{,}000$-row pilot corpus
using the real rule engine and replay verifier, accepting $9{,}039$ records in $34$
CPU minutes ($6{,}867$ replay-verified positives, $2{,}172$ exact-rationally
refuted negatives) and retaining $2{,}824$ rejections and $3{,}133$ typed failures.
It is reported honestly as \texttt{complete\_short} relative to the production
target rather than resampled (Table~\ref{tab:pilot}); its \texttt{test\_ood} split
holds only one accepted negative because the stress family offers few same-family,
size-matched neighbors, an imbalance we retain and report.

\subsection{Rewrite-step prediction}

Rewrite-step prediction uses one immutable $6$-arm $\times$ $3$-seed design: the
same goal-conditioned GNN over each graph channel, a compute-matched prefix
transformer, and a uniform policy sampled from the exact registry-derived
legal-action mask. Demonstration-action top-$k$ match is reported separately from
exact-successor-structure top-$k$ match, alongside the verifier-valid proposal
rate and complete attempted/valid/invalid/unsupported/timeout denominators.
Semantic equivalence alone is not counted as demonstration correctness.

\subsection{Proof and simplification}

The proof benchmark contains exactly $256$ frozen problems and the simplification
benchmark $1{,}000$ frozen expression identifiers. A proof succeeds only when it
reaches the exact target structural signature, its complete transition trace
replays, and its terminal claim is verifier-confirmed; lower cost, string
agreement, or an unsupported producer claim is not proof success. Uniform, policy,
policy-plus-value, and transformer methods share identical beam, expanded-node,
proof-depth, wall-time, and verifier-call budgets, and each unsuccessful row is
charged at least its expanded-node budget so crashes and timeouts cannot appear
artificially efficient. Simplification selects the lowest-cost valid state visited
under one preregistered exact \texttt{pure\_eml:official\_v4} cost.

\subsection{Symbolic regression}

The symbolic-regression benchmark comprises $256$ frozen synthetic tasks and a
restricted $32$-task Feynman-style subset~\cite{udrescu2020ai}, with a
$100$-formula exclusion ledger and verifier-confirmed recovery as the sole
criterion; the canonical manifests reproduced identically across three independent
CPU builds. The planned comparison includes EML-guided and AST-guided search, a
matched PySR baseline or labeled GP fallback, and a compact transformer-SR model
under frozen budgets and three seeds, reporting verifier-confirmed recovery over
both attempted and verifier-supported denominators alongside the
accuracy--complexity Pareto front rather than best accuracy alone.

\subsection{Frontier-LLM panel}
An external, verifier-normalized reference panel is implemented but is
\emph{external context only}: no row it produces may enter the controlled gates
G6--G11. The frozen workload is $100$ proof and $100$ SR tasks per model across
four providers ($800$ retained rows when a model completes), recording every
provider fact---model ID and snapshot, prompts and hashes, budgets, usage, cost,
raw responses, and verifier-confirmed correctness. No paid call has been made; the
panel ships with paid calls disabled and a zero-cost guard, and claimed
correctness is kept distinct from verifier-confirmed correctness.

succeeded on every row and produced a median tree expansion factor

\subsection{Exact compression is not competitiveness (C3)}
Lossless hash-consed sharing compresses expanded EML trees by a mean factor of
$39.3750$, yet the shared EML DAG remains larger than the \emph{uncompressed} AST
tree for every one of the $250{,}000$ expressions; the best remaining ratio is
exactly $8/7$.\anchor{docs/goals/goal3/GOAL3\_SUMMARY.md, ``Structural verdict'';
FINAL\_REPORT.md \#c3-dag-sharing-and-graph-costs.} The most compressible
expression shrinks by $28{,}087{,}277/180$ and still ends $540/13$ times larger
than its AST (mean EML-DAG/AST-tree $8.3344$, EML-DAG/AST-DAG $10.4750$).
``Compresses well'' and ``becomes structurally competitive'' are different claims,
and only the first is true here.

\subsection{Verified semantic rewriting helps a little, conditionally (C4)}
On a frozen, stratified $30{,}000$-expression selection, bounded equality
saturation with complete application provenance and independent candidate
verification improves $4{,}349/18{,}210$ costed rows ($23.882\%$) under
branch-insensitive real rewrites (\texttt{safe\_real}) and $5{,}026/18{,}210$
($27.600\%$) with guarded positive-real rules
(\texttt{positive\_real\_formal}), at mean relative savings of $2.361\%$ and
$2.849\%$ over costed rows and a largest single reduction of $302$ exact EML-DAG
nodes.\anchor{docs/goals/goal4/GOAL4\_SUMMARY.md, ``Production result'';
FINAL\_REPORT.md \#c4-e-graph-and-motif-compression.} Coverage is the binding
caveat: $11{,}566$ rows per mode ($38.553\%$) contain operators outside the closed
rewrite vocabulary (including \emph{all} trig/hyperbolic and mixed-elementary
rows), so these results hold on $60.700\%$ cost coverage and are conditional on
it. Each mode retains $224$ validation failures ($0.747\%$) and zero timeouts; the
source form is always a costed safety anchor, so improvement rates cannot be
inflated by dropped rows.

\subsection{Two preregistered nulls (C4)}
Both Goal~5 comparisons were published in the frozen null-before-positive
order.\anchor{docs/goals/goal5/GOAL5\_SUMMARY.md, ``Learned and neural
comparisons''.} First, a learned motif vocabulary did \emph{not} beat the
equal-budget frequent-motif vocabulary on TEST\_IID dictionary-inclusive MDL:
$324{,}485{,}346$ vs.\ $317{,}678{,}264$ bits over $25{,}000$ expressions (it did
beat the uncompressed macro baseline, $324{,}485{,}346$ vs.\ $572{,}524{,}716$,
and the median of $30$ fixed random vocabularies, $324{,}485{,}346$ vs.\
$368{,}943{,}015$). Second, a neural cost ranker did \emph{not} beat every
structural heuristic under the frozen comparison order: $8{,}349/18{,}050$
exact-best rankings vs.\ $8{,}796/18{,}050$ for the estimated-EML-tree-cost
heuristic (and $8{,}661/18{,}050$ for the AST-DAG-cost heuristic). On these tasks
the learned components add nothing over honest structural baselines: the
compression does not make the representation competitive, and the learned
machinery does not beat the transparent rules it was tested against.

\subsection{Learning tracks: a trainable representation, a collapsed guidance
signal}
\label{sec:pending}
The frozen apparatus has now produced its first authenticated verdicts on real
GPUs. Of the five tracks, two are measured (equivalence and the value head,
below), one is partial---rewrite-step at $13/18$ logical cells ($5$ invalid and
retained; separate retrieval grid $15/15$, zero authentication errors)---and
symbolic regression and the Pareto/Gate-G11 synthesis were not run and are scoped
as future work. The two measured tracks pull in opposite directions, and that
tension is the finding.

The equivalence track is a viability result. Trained purely on single-operator
EML-DAG source cells (three seeds, no AST comparison arm), two seeds reach
validation loss $0.5267$ and $0.5387$ BCE---below the $\ln 2\approx0.693$ chance
floor for binary equivalence---while the third diverges to $2.3914$. That a
$40\times$-expanded, maximally regular graph is learnable at all is evidence that
the representation's homogeneity is not by itself an obstacle to a graph model. It
is not evidence that EML \emph{helps}: the discriminative six-arm EML-vs-AST grid
was not run, and the encoder width and virtual-node choice stayed frozen at
\texttt{pending\_preflight\_freeze}. The viability question is answered; the
comparison stays open.

The value-head track is a diagnostic result, and a cautionary one
(Figure~\ref{fig:valuehead}). Under a reduced
budget ($1{,}500$ optimizer steps, three seeds, $452{,}820$ parameters, family
\texttt{exp\_log} held out), the goal-conditioned head reaches a validation MAE of
$0.830$--$0.847$ and a test-IID MAE near $1.87$--$1.92$. Read alone, that low error
looks like a working regressor. The ranking and out-of-distribution diagnostics say
otherwise: test-IID Spearman is $-0.014$, $0.002$, and $-0.009$ across seeds---
indistinguishable from zero---and the out-of-distribution predictor is
\emph{constant}, so its Spearman is undefined. The official review label is
\texttt{null\_or\_collapsed\_value\_head}: a guidance signal that MAE alone would
have passed and that only rank and OOD probes catch. We report it as the
methodological finding it is, not a working proof-search heuristic.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
The value-head collapse is not a footnote to the structural result; it is a
warning for neural-guided proof and symbolic-regression search, where a learned
value or cost head is routinely selected and reported by MAE or MSE. Ours reaches
a low validation MAE (${\approx}0.83$) yet ranks its own held-out states at chance
(test-IID Spearman ${\approx}0$) and degenerates to a constant out of
distribution: judged by the usual error metric it would have shipped as a working
heuristic. The concrete lesson is that regression error is not ranking utility,
and that a guidance model must be reported on rank correlation and
out-of-distribution behavior, not error alone---the diagnostics we release with
the apparatus. The three findings then compose into one account of the
single-operator representation: its structural cost is real and unrecovered by
sharing or rewriting, the representation is nonetheless trainable on binary
equivalence, but the guidance signal it was meant to supply collapses under
bounded compute. Regularity buys learnability without, on this evidence, buying
usable guidance---and that gap is where work on single-operator representations
should look next.

\section{Limitations, integrity, and reproducibility}
\label{sec:limits}

\paragraph{One scale.} Everything is measured at one fixed 250k-v1 scale; we fit
no scaling law and extrapolate to no other corpus, budget, or grammar. The
expressions are synthetic and generated, and the downstream benchmarks are
bounded, which limits external validity. With three seeds, planned learning
effects will be descriptive, not asymptotically significant.

\paragraph{Bounded semantic audit.} Numeric semantic verification covers a
deterministic sample, not the whole corpus. Across the original Goal~2 audit
($273$ rows) and a widened pilot audit ($2{,}988$ rows), the combined evidence
base is $3{,}261$ rows with $6$ retained mismatches
(${\approx}0.1\%$).\anchor{docs/goals/goal2/GOAL2\_SUMMARY.md, ``Semantic audit
and retained issues''.} The widened audit also hit one row whose numeric
materialization deterministically hung (reproduced twice), itself a
correctness-surface finding that motivates a per-row timeout. We claim structural
determinism corpus-wide but numeric verification only for the audited sample; no
mismatch is relabeled or resampled away.

\paragraph{Conditional coverage and equal-compute.} The e-graph results hold on
$60.7\%$ cost coverage, and the excluded families include trig/hyperbolic, the
worst-expansion family, so the semantic-compression numbers are not corpus-wide.
Because pure-EML expansion changes input size, equal parameters are not equal
compute in the learning tracks, and we report both sides.

\paragraph{Scope of Gate G10.} Gate G10's published fail bounds only the
grammar-v2 surface; it neither enables nor blocks any corpus or learning result
and stays unresolved by design. The frontier-LLM panel is external
context---proprietary data, nondeterminism, and model drift keep it out of every
controlled gate.

\paragraph{Artifacts and regeneration.} Production artifacts are stored
off-repository; the repository commits their SHA-256 digests, per-goal manifests,
exact CPU reproduction commands, and a single frozen lock file verified by a
CPU-only CI. Generation reproduces on commodity hardware (the $10{,}000$-row pilot
regenerated byte-identically twice, corpus hash \texttt{df4f3d74\ldots}), and the
$250$k stage refuses a machine below a twofold memory margin rather than running
degraded. Certification is incomplete and recorded as such: the binding machinery
authenticated zero artifacts in the archive's absence and committed a per-claim
pre-authentication digest index ($27$ of $28$ manifest entries missing) rather
than claiming certification it lacks.

\section{Conclusion}

At one fixed $250{,}000$-expression scale, the single-operator representation is
costly, trainable, and unable to guide. Its structural cost is real and
unrecovered---median $40.66\times$ expansion, every one of the $250{,}000$ shared
EML DAGs still larger than its \emph{uncompressed} AST tree, $2.4$--$2.8\%$
recovered by rewriting on $60.7\%$ coverage, and two nulls and a Gate~G10
\emph{fail} left unsoftened. Yet the representation is \emph{trainable}: two of
three equivalence seeds learn below the $\ln 2$ chance floor despite the
$40\times$ expansion. And the guidance signal \emph{collapses}: a value head with
low MAE (${\approx}0.83$) ranks at chance and goes constant out of
distribution---a failure MAE alone would hide. The contribution is honest
measurement, including where the signal fails, reported without resampling or
post-hoc adjustment.
